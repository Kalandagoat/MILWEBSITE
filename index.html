05 / HEALTH

Health

Good health is built through simple, consistent choices in our everyday lives.

HEALTH / WRITTEN WORK

Health Is Wealth: How Simple Habits Can Improve Our Everyday Lives

Health is one of the most important parts of our lives. There is a saying that our health is our wealth, no amount of money or material success can replace good health or buy it back once it is lost.

It is not only about being disease-free but also about taking care of our physical, mental, and emotional well-being. As students, we often become busy with school activities, assignments, examinations, and other responsibilities.

Because of this, we sometimes forget to take care of ourselves. We skip meals, sleep late, spend too much time on our phones, or ignore stress. And our everyday habits can affect our health in the present and in the future.

Eating nutritious food is One of the simplest ways to maintain good health. Our body needs different nutrients to grow, have energy, and function properly.

The World Health Organization (WHO, 2026) recommends eating a variety of foods, including fruits, vegetables, whole grains, and sources of protein.

It is also important to limit foods that contain too much sugar, salt, and unhealthy fats. Eating healthy does not mean that we can never enjoy our favorite foods. Instead, it means learning how to balance what we eat and making healthier choices most of the time.

According to the World Health Organization (WHO, 2024) Physical activity is another important part of a healthy lifestyle.

Exercise can help strengthen our muscles and bones, improve our fitness, and support our mental health.

Children and adolescents should get an average of at least 60 minutes of moderate-to-vigorous physical activity every day.

Physical activity does not always have to be a serious workout. Walking, dancing, playing sports, riding a bicycle, or even doing household chores can help us stay active.

Students experience different kinds of pressure, such as academic responsibilities, family problems, friendships, and personal struggles.

Sometimes, we may feel overwhelmed or emotionally tired. Taking care of our mental health is equally important.

According to the World Health Organization (WHO, 2024), anxiety and depression are among the common mental health concerns among adolescents.

This is why it is important to recognize our feelings and seek support when needed.

Talking to a trusted friend, family member, teacher, or professional can help us cope with difficult situations.

Getting enough rest is also essential. Sleep allows our bodies and minds to recover after a long day.

Unfortunately, many students sacrifice sleep to finish schoolwork or spend more time using their phones.

Although staying up late may seem harmless, constantly getting insufficient sleep can affect our energy, concentration, and overall well-being.

Creating a regular sleeping schedule and having 8 hours sleep can help us become more productive and feel better throughout the day.

Good health begins with small and consistent choices. We do not need to completely change our lifestyle overnight.

Eating nutritious food, staying physically active, getting enough sleep, managing stress, and asking for help are simple steps that can make a difference.

We should remember that taking care of our health is just as important as achieving good grades. Our health supports everything we want to accomplish in life.

By caring for ourselves today, we are also preparing ourselves for a healthier future.

ENVIRONMENT / WRITTEN WORK

How Simple Habits Can Protect Our Everyday World

The environment is basically the foundation of everything we do. There is a lot of truth to the old idea that our planet is our ultimate bank account.

No amount of money or success can patch up a broken ecosystem, and you cannot buy back clean air and fresh water once they are gone.

Keeping the planet healthy isn't just about stopping massive ecological disasters either. It is about how we treat our neighborhoods every single day.

As students, it is so easy to get completely buried under classes, assignments, and trying to have a social life.

When you are rushing around like that, you sort of forget how connected we are to everything around us.

We grab single-use plastics because we are in a hurry, leave lights on, or throw away things that still work.

But these little everyday habits add up fast, and they shape the exact world we have to live in right now and down the road.

Watching what we buy and throw away is one of the easiest ways to start looking out for the planet.

Earth only has so many resources to go around, and it needs them to stay balanced so wildlife can survive.

According to the United Nations Environment Programme (UNEP, 2025), moving toward a lifestyle where we reuse things and cut down on trash is the best way to stop global pollution.

It also means we should probably chill out on buying fast fashion or items wrapped in tons of plastic.

Living a bit greener doesn't mean you have to give up all your comfort. It just means pausing to think about what we waste and picking the eco-friendly option when we can.

Saving energy and water is another huge part of living a green life.

When we use less, we help slow down climate change, preserve wild areas, and protect animals.

The Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC, 2023) points out that simple things like saving water at home and using electricity wisely are incredibly powerful tools to cut down on emissions.

You do not need a massive lifestyle makeover to make a dent. Just turn off your computer when you are done, shut off the faucet while brushing your teeth, take quicker showers, or take the bus and carpool with friends instead of driving alone.

Being a student right now brings a ton of pressure, from fighting for good grades to feeling genuinely stressed out about the future of the world.

Feeling completely overwhelmed by huge environmental issues is a totally normal thing to go through.

In fact, the United Nations Educational, Scientific and Cultural Organization (UNESCO, 2024) found that eco-anxiety is a growing mental health issue for young people everywhere.

That is why it helps so much to turn that stress into small, local actions.

Joining a campus cleanup crew, planting trees, or even just venting about these worries with friends and teachers can make you feel less helpless because you are working on shared solutions.

We also need to pay attention to how much time we spend around nature.

Spending a little time outside helps us appreciate the world we are trying to save, and it gives your brain a much-needed break.

Unfortunately, a lot of us spend almost all day indoors, staring at phones and laptops, totally cut off from the outside world.

Just hanging out in a park, keeping a couple of plants in your room, or helping out in a school garden can make you care a lot more about protecting the earth.

Fixing the planet starts with small, daily choices.

References

United Nations Environment Programme (UNEP, 2025).

Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC, 2023).

United Nations Educational, Scientific and Cultural Organization (UNESCO, 2024).
